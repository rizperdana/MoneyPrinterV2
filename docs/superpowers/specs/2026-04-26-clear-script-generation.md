# Clear Script Generation - Design Spec

## Problem

Current script generation produces confusing, nonsensical scripts that even the topic expert can't understand. Scripts jump around, lack clear explanation, and don't answer "so what?" at the end.

## Target Audience

General audience (12+) who are curious but not specialists. Smart people wanting to understand new topics quickly.

## Solution

### 4-Step Clear Structure (Approach A)

Every script MUST follow this exact order:

#### Step 1: SETUP (1 sentence)
- Hook with ONE surprising fact about the topic
- Grab attention immediately
- Stay on ONE point

#### Step 2: DISCOVERY (2-3 sentences)  
- What happened / What is it / The facts
- Be specific with numbers, names, dates
- No jumping between topics

#### Step 3: EXPLANATION (2-3 sentences)
- WHY this matters / WHY it works
- Use simple analogies: "like [something everyone knows]"
- Skip jargon or explain in 3 words max

#### Step 4: TAKEAWAY (1-2 sentences)
- MUST answer "so what?" or "why should I care?"
- Use: "That's why..." or "...and here's what this means"
- Viewer leaves SMARTER, not just curious

## Examples

### Bad script (current problem):
"Hearts were found in a basement. There were 3,000 of them. Some were beating. The judge was strange. Medical stuff happened. It's creepy. Be scared."

### Good script (new approach):  
"In the 1970s, investigators found 3,000 hearts preserved in jars in a judge's basement. The judge was a medical examiner collecting hearts from autopsies for decades. Some hearts were still weakly beating outside the body. That's why this matters — studying hearts helped doctors find diseases running in families so relatives could get treatment in time."

## Rules Summary

1. **Follow order:** SETUP → DISCOVERY → EXPLANATION → TAKEAWAY
2. **One topic:** ONE point, not five
3. **New info each sentence:** No filler
4. **End with takeaway:** Always answer "so what?"
5. **Simple words:** Explain or replace any jargon

## Test Script

Topic: "The Mary Toft rabbit story"
> SETUP: In 1700s England, a young woman claimed she gave birth to only rabbits.  
> DISCOVERY: Mary Toft convinced doctors she was pregnant with bunnies. King George I himself got curious and had her examined.  
> EXPLANATION: This wasn't magic — it was "maternal impression," the old belief that a pregnant woman's cravings and thoughts could shape her baby. Doctors eventually proved she was lying to escape poverty.  
> TAKEAWAY: That's why historical medical mysteries matter — they show how doctors learned to separate real science from wishful thinking.
